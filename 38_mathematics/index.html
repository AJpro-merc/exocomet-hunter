    38. Mathematics¶

    Status: reflects repo state as of 2026-09-16, commit 01c2051. Every formula below is transcribed directly from the implementation (detect/baseline.py, detect/candidates.py, detect/asymmetry.py, detect/model_comparison.py, detect/scoring.py) — not a textbook-generic version of the same idea. Where the implementation departs from the "obvious" formula (e.g. truncated rather than padded windows), that departure is documented, because it was a deliberate, tested choice.

    38.1 Median absolute deviation (MAD) → robust sigma, detect/baseline.py¶

    Local baseline is a centered rolling median (rolling_median, backed by pandas.Series.rolling(window, center=True, min_periods=1).median()), not a mean — a median is insensitive to the minority of strongly deviant points that make up an in-transit dip.

    Robust per-cadence noise (local_noise) is computed as:

    $$\text{MAD}i = \text{median}\big(|{\text{flux} - \text{baseline}}|\big){\text{window centered on } i}$$

    $$\sigma_i = 1.4826 \times \text{MAD}_i$$

    The constant MAD_TO_SIGMA = 1.4826 is the standard factor converting a MAD into a Gaussian-equivalent standard deviation, exactly as coded (no variant scaling). A floor is applied: floor = max(global_sigma * 1e-3, np.finfo(np.float64).tiny), where global_sigma = 1.4826 * median(|flux - baseline|) computed once over the whole series — this prevents a zero denominator on perfectly flat synthetic or masked stretches.

    Windows are truncated, not padded, at series edges (rolling(min_periods=1) — any available points, not a padded value). The module docstring states why this specific choice was tested and kept: padding with a repeated edge value "inflated the apparent significance of pure noise at the array edges to 30-70 sigma — far above a genuine injected dip." This is a real, tested finding recorded in the source itself, not a design assumption.

    38.2 Physical-time-to-cadence conversion, detect/baseline.py¶

    Every window in config/thresholds.yaml is specified in days, converted to a cadence count at runtime:

    $$n_{\text{cadences}} = \max!\left(\text{round}!\left(\frac{\text{days}}{\text{cadence_days}}\right),\ 1\right)$$

    then coerced to odd and clamped to the series length by odd_window. This exists specifically because Kepler's cadence (29.4 min) and TESS's (20 s–1800 s depending on product) differ by more than two orders of magnitude — see Next Steps.md item A1.

    38.3 Depth significance and candidate identification, detect/candidates.py¶

    Per-cadence dimming significance:

    $$s_i = \frac{\text{baseline}_i - \text{flux}_i}{\sigma_i}$$

    positive for a dimming, negative for a brightening (depth_significance). A cadence is "in-dip" when $s_i > \texttt{sigma_threshold}$ (default 4.0, config/thresholds.yaml). Runs of at least min_consecutive (default 3) such cadences are kept; runs separated by at most max_gap_cadences (default 2) are merged. Each surviving run is then expanded outward, cadence by cadence, while $s_i$ remains above recovery_sigma (default 1.0) and the total window stays within max_window_days (default 3.0 days) — a direct, unbounded-in-code but config-bounded, linear-time loop (_expand_window).

    38.4 Sub-cadence minimum location, detect/asymmetry.py::locate_minimum¶

    A parabola is fit through the deepest cadence and its two neighbors ($f_{-1}, f_0, f_{+1}$ at times $t_{-1}, t_0, t_{+1}$). With $D = f_{-1} - 2f_0 + f_{+1}$:

    $$\delta = \frac{0.5\,(f_{-1} - f_{+1})}{D}, \qquad t_{\min} = t_0 + \delta \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}(t_{+1} - t_{-1}), \qquad f_{\min} = f_0 - 0.25\,(f_{-1} - f_{+1})\,\delta$$

    used only when $D > 0$ (a genuine local minimum, i.e. concave-up) and $-1 < \delta < 1$ (vertex falls inside the central cadence); otherwise the discrete minimum is returned unchanged. This is the standard three-point parabolic-vertex interpolation, applied to give the reported $t_{\min}$ sub-cadence precision.

    38.5 Duration and slope asymmetry, detect/asymmetry.py::compute_asymmetry¶

    Each side's half-depth crossing is found by walking outward from the minimum until flux recovers to half_level = baseline_level - half_depth_fraction * depth (default half_depth_fraction = 0.5), then linearly interpolating between the bracketing cadences. Each side's slope is an ordinary-least-squares fit of flux against time over the cadences between that crossing and the minimum (ols_slope, standard closed-form OLS with slope_err from residual variance, inf if fewer than 3 points or no spread in $x$).

    With dur_ingress, dur_egress the two side durations (crossing → minimum) and slope_ingress, slope_egress their OLS gradients:

    $$A_{dur} = \frac{\text{dur}{egress} - \text{dur}{ingress}}{\text{dur}{egress} + \text{dur}{ingress}}, \qquad A_{slope} = \frac{|\text{slope}{ingress}| - |\text{slope}{egress}|}{|\text{slope}{ingress}| + |\text{slope}{egress}|}$$

    Both lie in $[-1, 1]$ and are positive for a fast-fade, slow-recovery (comet-like) event by construction — $A_{slope}$ deliberately places the ingress term first so that "the two metrics agree in sign" is a direct statement about physical consistency (AsymmetryResult.signs_agree: sign(a_dur) == sign(a_slope) and a_dur != 0).

    Known limitation of this contour-based pair, documented in research log 003: at the half-depth contour specifically, a Gaussian ingress of width $\sigma$ reaches it at $1.177\sigma$ and an exponential egress of timescale $\tau$ reaches it at $0.693\tau$. Setting these equal gives the sign-flip point:

    $$\frac{\tau}{\sigma}\Big|_{\text{sign flip}} = \frac{1.177}{0.693} \approx 1.70$$

    So a transit with $1 < \tau/\sigma < 1.70$ has an unambiguously longer tail yet yields a negative $A_{dur}$ — the half-depth contour samples the nearly-symmetric core, not the extended wing where the comet signature actually lives. This is why $\tau/\sigma$ (from the parametric fit, §38.6) rather than $A_{dur}$ is the primary shape statistic; $A_{dur}$/$A_{slope}$ remain reported, interpretable, model-independent cross-checks.

    38.6 Parametric profiles and BIC model comparison, detect/model_comparison.py¶

    Symmetric (null) model — Gaussian dip:

    $$f(t) = b - d \cdot \exp!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{t - t_0}{w}\right)^2\right)$$

    with $b$ = baseline, $d$ = depth, $w$ = Gaussian width (floored at _MIN_WIDTH_DAYS = 1e-4 days to prevent collapse onto a single cadence).

    Comet model — Gaussian ingress joined continuously to an exponential egress at $t_0$:

    $$f(t) = \begin{cases} b - d \cdot \exp!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{t - t_0}{\sigma}\right)^2\right) & t \le t_0 \[4pt] b - d \cdot \exp!\left(-\dfrac{t - t_0}{\tau}\right) & t > t_0 \end{cases}$$

    Both halves reach $b - d$ continuously at $t_0$ by construction; $\sigma$ = ingress Gaussian width, $\tau$ = egress e-folding timescale.

    Fitting: bounded nonlinear least squares (scipy.optimize.least_squares, method="trf", max_nfev=2000) minimizing $\sum_i \left(\frac{f(t_i) - y_i}{\sigma_{y,i}}\right)^2$ over the event window widened by padding_factor (default 1.5×) so the recovering tail is not truncated. Requires at least 8 cadences in the padded window (else returns None — "too few cadences to constrain five free parameters").

    BIC (ModelFit.bic, exact code):

    $$\text{BIC} = \chi^2 + k \ln(n)$$

    where $\chi^2 = \sum_i \left(\frac{\text{model}(t_i) - y_i}{\sigma_{y,i}}\right)^2$ (the least-squares objective's sum of squared, error-weighted residuals — i.e. this is $\chi^2$ in the weighted-residuals sense, not a raw sum of squared flux differences), $k$ = number of free parameters (4 for symmetric, 5 for comet), and $n$ = number of cadences fitted. Returns $+\infty$ if the fit did not converge or $n \le 0$.

    ΔBIC (ModelComparison.delta_bic):

    $$\Delta\text{BIC} = \text{BIC}{\text{symmetric}} - \text{BIC}{\text{comet}}$$

    Positive favors the comet model; the comet profile's extra free parameter is charged the $k \ln n$ penalty so it must earn its added flexibility. favors_comet is true when $\Delta\text{BIC} > \texttt{delta_bic_threshold}$ (default 10.0 — the conventional "strong evidence" cutoff, cited to Kass & Raftery 1995 in research log 007).

    τ/σ (ModelComparison.tau_over_sigma):

    $$\frac{\tau}{\sigma} = \frac{\text{egress_tau}}{\text{ingress_width}}$$

    read directly from the converged comet-model fit parameters; nan if either is non-finite or $\sigma \le 0$. Values above 1 indicate a trailing tail. This is the primary shape statistic precisely because it is derived from the whole fitted profile rather than a single contour level (see §38.5's sign-flip discussion).

    38.7 The flagging rule, detect/scoring.py::score_event¶

    $$\text{flagged} = \big(\Delta\text{BIC} \text{ finite}\big) \wedge \big(\tau/\sigma \text{ finite}\big) \wedge \big(\Delta\text{BIC} > \texttt{delta_bic_threshold}\big) \wedge \big(\tau/\sigma > \texttt{tau_over_sigma_threshold}\big)$$

    with defaults delta_bic_threshold = 10.0 and tau_over_sigma_threshold = 1.0 (config/thresholds.yaml). Both conditions are required — research log 007 demonstrates concretely (with a mirror-image synthetic pair, $\Delta\text{BIC} = 201$ for the physically-backwards event versus $151$ for the real comet, while $\tau/\sigma = 0.23$ versus $4.15$) that $\Delta\text{BIC}$ alone cannot distinguish tail direction; only $\tau/\sigma$ does.

    38.8 Bootstrap uncertainty estimation, detect/scoring.py::bootstrap_a_dur_sigma¶

    For draws iterations (default 1000 in production, 300 in generate_training_labels.py for throughput, 60 in the fast test config): add Gaussian noise scaled by each cadence's own error bar to the flux inside and immediately around the event window,

    $$\text{flux}'i = \text{flux}_i + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma{\text{err},i}), \quad i \in [\text{start}-1, \text{end}+1]$$

    then re-run the full ingress/egress measurement (measure_event) and record the resulting $A_{dur}$ if measurable. The bootstrap standard deviation, np.std(samples, ddof=1) (sample standard deviation, Bessel-corrected), is a_dur_sigma. Significance is then:

    $$\text{significance} = \frac{|A_{dur}|}{\sigma_{A_{dur}}}$$

    with the special cases: nan sigma → significance 0; sigma $\le 0$ (noiseless synthetic data) → significance $\infty$ if $A_{dur} \ne 0$ else 0. This significance value is reported but, per the current flagging rule (§38.7), does not gate the flagging decision — it is retained as an output column and a classifier feature (FEATURE_NAMES includes significance and a_dur_sigma).

    38.9 Periodicity test, detect/scoring.py::periodicity_flag¶

    astropy.timeseries.LombScargle(time, flux, flux_err).autopower() over the frequency range corresponding to periodicity_min_period_days–periodicity_max_period_days (0.5–50 days, config/thresholds.yaml). The peak power's false-alarm probability (model.false_alarm_probability) is compared against periodicity_fap_threshold (default 0.01); below this, the light curve is flagged periodic. This is standard Lomb-Scargle periodogram analysis via Astropy's own implementation — no custom periodogram math is implemented in this codebase. Used only as a ranking penalty, never a hard rejection.
